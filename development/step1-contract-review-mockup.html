Arbeitsvertrag — CJ Foods Europe GmbH Example clauses, EN translation

§1  Commencement of Employment

The employment relationship begins on 1 September 2026 and is for an indefinite period.

§2  Remuneration

The Employee shall receive an annual gross salary of EUR 50,500, payable in twelve equal monthly instalments.

§3  Probationary Period

The first six (6) months of employment constitute a probationary period, during which either party may terminate with two weeks' notice.

§4  Annual Leave

The Employee is entitled to 25 working days of paid annual leave per calendar year.

§5  Notice Period

Following the probationary period, the Employee may terminate this agreement with three (3) months' notice to the end of a calendar month; the Employer may terminate with the statutory minimum notice applicable to the Employee's length of service.

— no further clauses in this draft —

7 findings · tap a card or a marker to link them
1Start date is unambiguousConfirmed
A fixed calendar start date with no conditions attached. Nothing to chase here.
2Salary sits €200 under the Blue Card thresholdFix before signing
€50,500 falls just short of the 2026 EU Blue Card general threshold of €50,700 — at this figure, the visa route itself is at risk, not just your take-home pay. A raise of just €200/year clears it. Ask for €51,000+ to leave margin, since the threshold is reviewed annually and typically rises each year. (If this role could be classified as a shortage occupation — MINT/STEM/healthcare — the bar drops to €45,934, which you'd already clear; worth asking HR.)
→ Ask for a small, easy bump — this alone unlocks Blue Card eligibility
3Probation period is standardConfirmed
Six months matches the typical German Probezeit. Two-week mutual notice during this window is also standard — not a red flag.
4Vacation exceeds the legal minimumConfirmed
25 days is above the statutory floor of 20. Note: Hessen's 5 Bildungsurlaub days are a separate statutory entitlement — don't let these 25 get counted against them.
5Notice period is asymmetricWorth checking
You'd owe 3 months' notice; the employer's obligation isn't spelled out beyond "statutory minimum." Worth confirming this asymmetry is within normal bounds — a Rechtsschutzversicherung (legal insurance) makes this kind of check cheap later.
6No Weihnachtsgeld clause foundMissing
Christmas bonus is never a legal default in Germany — if it isn't written down, it doesn't exist. Ask HR to confirm and add it in writing before you sign; if paid 3 years running it becomes a standing right.
→ Generate a clause-request email
7No relocation package mentionedMissing
Nothing in this draft covers moving costs, visa support, or temporary housing. For a Korea→Germany senior move, packages of €20,000–50,000 aren't unusual — ask for at least a €10,000–15,000 lump sum if nothing is offered.
→ Add to your counter-offer draft
